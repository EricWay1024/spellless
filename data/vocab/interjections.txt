# Short words the base frequency list simply does not contain.
#
# `oh` is not a rare word.  It is not in `frequency_dictionary_en_82_765.txt`
# at all -- nor are `ah`, `eh`, `uh`, `um`, `hm`, `ok`, `ya` or `yo` -- while
# `hey`, `yeah`, `wow`, `hmm`, `huh` and `okay` all are.  Whatever produced
# that list dropped the two-letter interjections, and the effect on the matcher
# is worse than a missing word usually is: with nothing to match exactly,
# typing `oh` offered `on`, `of`, `how`, `the` and put `oh` itself in the
# literal slot at the bottom of the page.
#
# So these are not new vocabulary, they are a gap being filled.  The ranks
# below are set against words the corpus *does* carry -- `yeah` at 2,933,
# `okay` at 4,323, `hmm` at 10,209 -- so that each entry sits about where it
# would have sat had the list not lost it.
#
# What is deliberately not here: `lol`, `omg`, `btw`, `fyi`, `imo`, `tbh`.
# Those are shorthand rather than words, and shorthand.txt says where personal
# shorthand belongs -- your own spellless_shortcuts.txt, not the dictionary.

#!rank 1200
# The commonest interjection in written English by a wide margin.
oh

#!rank 3000
ah
ok

#!rank 6000
# "A vs B" is written without the stop at least as often as with it, and
# academic prose is full of both.
vs

# not distort them they lose to `we` and `oil`, so the entry buys nothing and
# only adds a rival to other queries.
yay
